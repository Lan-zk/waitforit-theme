The green notebook came back. I had given it up for lost in April, when the rain got into the satchel and I decided the sea had a better claim on it than I did. Then on Tuesday it was on the counter at the post office, damp at the corners, smelling faintly of rope, with a note inside that said: found this on the rocks. Nothing else. No name, no date.

I have been reading it slowly, a few pages at a time, the way you read something from someone you were once close to. Half of it is lists. Things to do, things to remember, things to buy in town. The other half is weather. I did not remember writing down so much weather. Apparently it mattered, then.

The harbour lights are doing their slow rounds again. Everyone in the town says they are automated now, and everyone in the town still looks up when they turn. I have decided this is the most honest thing about us: we know the machinery, and we look up anyway.

The tide said nothing, in case you are wondering. But it came in from the south all evening, which is not the direction it usually comes from, and I thought you would want to know.
